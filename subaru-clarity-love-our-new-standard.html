Page Insight Report · Love Our New Standard

Love Our New Standard

A focused review of /love-our-new-standard/, the maintenance plan campaign page, using Microsoft Clarity session data, heatmaps and error logs alongside Gravity Forms entry data. The page is performing adequately: stripping bot traffic, it converts at 1.6%, matching the healthiest lead form on the site. The limiting factor is exposure rather than the form itself. The enquiry form begins at 76.5% depth on a 6,556px page — past the point where half of mobile visitors have already left — and completing it requires reaching 85–90% depth, where 13% remain. Mobile load time on the engaged segment also reaches 6.98s and appears to be causing the page’s dead clicks. Four changes are recommended. Two further issues — a third of sessions being automated, and no enquiries since 9 September — sit outside development scope and are noted for the client.

Section 01 · Context

Reading these numbers

i

Scale and method

This page carries 453 sessions against 56,835 site-wide, so individual figures are directional rather than statistically firm. Heatmap data covers 372 mobile and 100 desktop pageviews. The earliest recorded form entry is dated 31 July, so the form entry record covers roughly eight weeks of the ninety-day analysis window.

Clarity project SubaruSA, production domain only. Gravity Forms entries used to validate behaviour. Of the eleven entries recorded in the period, six were internal test submissions; these have since been removed and are excluded from the conversion figure throughout.

Findings

Five findings, ordered by how much each limits the page

The sessions producing dead clicks are markedly slower — almost seven seconds to largest contentful paint on mobile. Combined with zero rage clicks and only four JavaScript errors across all 30 sessions in this segment, the most probable explanation is visitors tapping during load, before event handlers have bound. Nothing on the page is broken; it is not ready when they touch it.

These are also the page's most engaged visitors — four pages and nearly six minutes against a page average of 2.52 pages and 92 seconds of active time. The slow experience is landing on the people most likely to enquire.
Sample size is 30 sessions, so this is directional rather than conclusive — but reducing mobile load time is likely to remove most of these dead clicks without any element-level work.

The cliff is at 75%, and the form starts at 76.5%. Measured on the live page, the enquiry form begins at 76.5% depth on a total page height of 6,556px — one and a half points past the point where the page begins losing visitors rapidly. Between 75% and 85% depth the page loses 74% of everyone still reading, and because the form occupies roughly the final 1,500px, completing it requires reaching 85–90% depth, where only 13% of visitors remain.

In practical terms: around 175 of 372 mobile visitors see where the form starts, and roughly 50 get far enough down to fill it in. Against that, 10 submissions is most of the people who had a realistic opportunity.

A fifth of visitors leave in the first 15% of the page. 370 reach 10% depth and only 295 reach 15%. That is where someone decides whether the page is what they were looking for, and it aligns with the 15.67% quick-back rate. Given 6.98s mobile LCP on the engaged segment, some of this is visitors leaving before the page has finished rendering.
3

The form takes a long time to complete

Opportunity
Median submission speed is 39 seconds, with one entry at 152 seconds. For a form collecting a name, email, phone number and dealer selection, that is slow — consistent with the chained province and dealership selects being laborious on a small screen. Mobile is 65% of this page's traffic.

This is worth fixing on its own merits, but it is not the main reason enquiries are low. As Finding 2 shows, most visitors never reach the form — so the 72 Request quote events against 10 submissions is largely a question of exposure rather than form friction. Reducing completion effort compounds the benefit once more people arrive at the form; it does not substitute for getting them there.

These visitors are not struggling to find vehicles. That pattern indicates an unanswered question about coverage — which models the plan applies to, and what it includes for theirs. They are leaving to look up something the page does not tell them.

Dead clicks run at 12.80% and quick backs at 15.67%, both roughly double the site average. Rage clicks are zero, so visitors try something once, get nothing, and back out rather than persisting.
5

Conversion is healthy — the page is not broken

Confirmed
Five genuine enquiries from 311 human sessions is 1.6%, the same rate as the Book a Test Drive form identified as the site's benchmark in CLARITY-01. Ten sessions fired the Submit form event and eleven entries were recorded, so submission works end to end. A test submission confirmed the form is functioning correctly. Dealer and province capture correctly.

This sits alongside the findings above rather than contradicting them. The visitors who reach the form complete it successfully — but fewer reach it than should, and the friction described above is what limits that number. The opportunity on this page is getting more people to the form, not fixing the form itself.
!

Two items outside this report's scope

For the client
No enquiries since 9 September

Entries ran at six in July, four in August and one in September, with the last on 9 September. The form has been tested and works, so this is not a technical fault — it points to a change in the volume or quality of traffic reaching the page. Worth establishing whether anything changed in the second week of September. This is the single largest influence on lead volume from this page and it is not a development issue.

Nearly a third of sessions are automated

142 of 453 sessions are flagged as bots, against roughly 14% site-wide, with ten carrying a PPC ad fraud flag specifically. Notably, the engaged segment is almost clean — only two flagged sessions out of 30 — so the automated traffic is not distorting the behavioural findings above. Worth confirming with whoever manages paid media which networks are sending traffic here.

Also noted

The page records the same globally-loaded JavaScript faults documented site-wide in CLARITY-01. No page-specific remediation is required; those fixes are already listed there.

Section 03 · Actions

Recommendations

Four changes, ordered by value returned against effort

1

Add a sticky mobile CTA linking to the form

Highest value
The form begins at 76.5% depth on a 6,556px page — past the point where 51% of mobile visitors have already left
Completing it requires reaching roughly 85–90% depth, where only 13% of visitors remain
A persistent "Get a quote" bar anchoring to the form gives every visitor a one-tap route from wherever they decide
Repeat the CTA in or below the hero and after each major content block, so the offer follows each answered question
Do not move the form itself — see the note below
·

Why not simply move the form up the page

The obvious response to a form at 76.5% is to move it higher, but the scroll data argues against that. The curve is almost flat between 20% and 70% depth — 273 visitors down to 214 — which means the content between the hero and the form is being read rather than skipped. A maintenance plan is a considered purchase, and coverage, duration and pricing are reasonably wanted before a visitor hands over contact details. Interrupting a sequence that is holding attention risks fewer and lower-quality enquiries.

The problem is not the form's position. It is that a visitor who has decided at 30% depth has no way to act on that decision without scrolling through content they no longer need. A sticky CTA solves that without disturbing the page.
2

Reduce mobile load time

Medium
6.98s LCP and 440ms INP on the sessions producing dead clicks
Most probable cause of the 12.80% dead-click rate — visitors tapping before handlers bind
Affects the page's most engaged visitors, who view four pages and stay nearly six minutes
Benefits from the template-level work already recommended in CLARITY-01
3

Simplify the dealer selection

Medium
39-second median completion on a four-field form
Chained province and dealership selects are the likely cause on mobile
Consider removing dealer selection entirely and routing by province, or replacing the two dependent dropdowns with a single searchable field
4

Answer the vehicle question on the page

Content
Add plan coverage by model near the form — a table or tabs showing model, plan length and what is included
Place it above the 75% depth mark alongside the form
Visitors arriving from a model page can then get their answer here instead of navigating back
If a browse-vehicles section is wanted, it belongs below the form as an exit route for non-converting visitors — never above it, where it competes with the enquiry
